Five instant loan apps Indians actually use, compared

SUVRON Money team · 8 min read

Search for an instant loan app in India and you'll get dozens of results, most of them written by the apps themselves. Here's a straighter comparison of five names that keep coming up, what each one is actually built for, and what to check before you download any of them. Rates, limits and tenure on lending apps change often and vary by lender policy and your own credit profile, so treat the ranges below as a starting point, not a quote, and always confirm the current numbers and full Key Fact Statement on the app itself before applying.

Fibe

Formerly EarlySalary, Fibe is built around salary-advance style borrowing for salaried professionals. It's positioned for speed, with same-day disbursal and loan amounts that scale up from small salary advances to larger personal loans, on tenures typically running from a few months out to a few years. It works best if you have a steady payslip and want a fast, mostly digital process.

KreditBee

KreditBee built its reputation on accessibility for young professionals and first-time borrowers, including people with a thin or non-existent credit history. It typically offers smaller loan amounts on shorter tenures for new applicants, with the option to build up to larger amounts and longer tenures as you repay on time. The tradeoff for that accessibility is usually a wider interest rate range, so a first loan through KreditBee is often priced higher than what an established borrower would get elsewhere.

CASHe

CASHe uses what it calls a Social Loan Quotient rather than relying purely on a CIBIL score, pulling in alternative data to assess applicants who don't have a long credit history. That makes it a common option for people just entering the formal credit system. Loan sizes tend to be smaller and tenures shorter than a standard personal loan, which suits it well to genuine short-term needs rather than large purchases.

mPokket

mPokket is built specifically for students and very early-career borrowers, a segment most lenders don't serve at all because there's no income history to underwrite against. Loan amounts are small and tenures are short by design. It's a useful option if you're a student with a genuine short-term gap, but it's not built for larger borrowing needs.

MoneyView

MoneyView runs its own credit model rather than only using a bureau score, which lets it work with applicants who have a lower CIBIL score than most lenders would accept. It covers a wider loan range than the smaller apps on this list, with tenures that stretch out further, making it a middle ground between short-term small-ticket apps and larger term loans.

What actually matters before you pick one

Every one of these apps will show you a starting interest rate on its marketing page. That rate is almost always reserved for borrowers with a strong credit score and stable income, and your actual rate can land well above it. The number worth comparing isn't the headline rate, it's the Key Fact Statement you get after you apply: the exact APR, all fees, the total repayment amount, and any charges for early repayment or a missed EMI.

Beyond the numbers, check two things for any lending app before you download it. First, whether it's RBI-registered or partners with an RBI-registered NBFC, since that's the baseline for legitimate lending in India. Second, whether there's an actual support channel you can reach if something on your repayment schedule looks wrong, not just an automated chat that goes in circles. A fast approval is worth very little if getting help afterward is slow.
